# 📦 Reflection – Product Backlog

## ⭐ STAR Method Reflection

---

### ⭐ Situation

During the development of PowerShare, a portable power station rental platform, the team needed to organise the requirements for customers and administrators. The platform includes features such as account registration, login, battery browsing, rental bookings, notifications, and administrative management.

---

### 🎯 Task

My responsibility as the Full-Stack Developer was to understand the technical requirements and contribute to organising the features into a product backlog. The backlog needed to identify the required functionality, establish priorities, and provide a clear direction for development.

---

### ⚙️ Action

I organised the main PowerShare features into product backlog items and described them as user stories. I considered which features were essential to the customer journey, particularly registration, login, browsing batteries, and making rental bookings. I also included administrative requirements for managing batteries, customers, bookings, and reports.

I used priorities to distinguish important core features from supporting functionality and added acceptance criteria to explain what each feature should achieve. This helped me understand how backlog items can guide development and provide a basis for checking whether requirements have been met.

---

### ✅ Result

Creating the product backlog helped me understand how Agile teams organise requirements and prioritise work according to product goals and user needs. It also showed me the importance of writing clear acceptance criteria so that the team can understand what is expected from each feature.

One lesson I learned is that a backlog should remain accurate as the application develops. In future projects, I would review priorities regularly with the team, refine acceptance criteria before implementation, and connect completed backlog items to evidence such as working features, tests, and GitHub commits.

Overall, this activity improved my understanding of product planning and how the product backlog connects user requirements to sprint planning and software development.

---

### 💡 Reflection and Learning

This activity taught me that an effective product backlog helps keep development organised and focused on user needs. I learned that prioritisation, clear acceptance criteria, and regular refinement are important for ensuring that the team understands what needs to be developed.

As the Full-Stack Developer, I also recognised the importance of connecting backlog items to the actual implementation of the application. In future projects, I would work more closely with the team to review requirements, update priorities when necessary, and use evidence such as screenshots, tests, and GitHub commits to demonstrate progress.
